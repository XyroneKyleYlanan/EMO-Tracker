5.3 Running it and computing the results

  1. Before: the adviser and the prof check all three forms. Print, for each user, their role's task sheet, the SUS and the ISO/IEC 25010 questionnaire; print the facilitator guide for the observers.
  2. At the session: follow the facilitator guide (page 1 of the usability test). One proponent reads the tasks and keeps time; another writes. Write the real date on every form.
  3. After: type everything into evaluation-scoring.xlsx (Excel or Google Sheets): the Usability test, SUS, ISO ratings, Respondents and Comments tabs. It computes everything; check the numbers against the paper forms.

Usability test: completion rate = tasks completed without help ÷ tasks recorded × 100 (the sheet also shows the rate with help). Report the average time and the average ease rating, read on the scale below.

Rating scale (ease ratings and ISO/IEC 25010): use frequency, percentage and the weighted mean (slide 16). With three respondents, don't use other statistical tests.

Percentage: P = f ÷ n × 100

Weighted mean: x̄ = Σfx ÷ n

Here f is how many gave a rating, x is the rating and n is the number of respondents. Example: ratings of 5, 4 and 5 give 14 ÷ 3 = 4.67, Strongly agree. A characteristic's mean is the average of its statements' means; the overall mean is the average of the characteristic means.

3.41 to 4.20AgreeVery good
2.61 to 3.40NeutralGood

1.00 to 1.80Strongly disagreePoor

Ask the adviser which labels she wants.

SUS score = 2.5 × [ Σ(odd-statement rating − 1) + Σ(5 − even-statement rating) ]

The score is computed for each respondent (0 to 100; it is not a percentage), then averaged. A score of 68 is the usual average, already cited in Chapter 2 (Hyzy et al., 2022). Comments: type them into the Comments tab word for word, make categories (for example ease of use, speed, missing features, problems), label each comment and write the patterns. Don't turn comments into numbers (slide 15).
