2. The Routine for Each Part

Use these steps inside Parts 2 to 5 (the Overview and Objectives 1 to 3).

Step 1: Write the bold objective sentence

  1. Take the objective from Chapter 1.
  2. Copy it word for word and drop the opening word "To".
  3. Put it in bold, on its own line, right before the first figure of that part.
  4. For the Overview, use your General Objective in the same way.
Example with Objective 2 (word for word) Chapter 1: "To develop a readiness classification feature that uses three rule-based readiness labels: On Track, At Risk, Critical and shows the reason for each label..."
Chapter 4 (bold): "Develop a readiness classification feature that uses three rule-based readiness labels: On Track, At Risk, Critical and shows the reason for each label..."
Your objectives are too long to copy A bold sentence works best when it is one sentence. Objective 1 has 8 sentences, and Objectives 2 and 3 have 3 each. In Chapter 1, you can keep one sentence as the objective and move the details (the fields, the statuses, the lists) to the Scope. That one sentence must still name every feature that its part shows: Objective 2's first sentence covers readiness only, and Analytics is in its second sentence, so the shortened sentence needs both. In the General Objective, "Schedule" can be lowercase, since it means the office's schedule in general, not the Schedule page.

Step 2: Show each feature, one at a time

  1. Insert the screenshot.
  2. Add the caption, for example Figure 4.5: Schedule page.
  3. Write a paragraph after it that starts with "Figure 4.5 shows..."
  4. In the paragraph, say what the screen is, what the user can do, and one detail that matters (a rule, a color, or a warning).
  5. Keep each paragraph to 3 to 5 sentences.
Example frame with the double-booking warning Figure 4.7 shows the new event form after a booking that overlaps another one was entered. [What the user can do here.] The warning appears when two bookings are at the same venue and room at overlapping times, and saving is still allowed.

Step 3: Add the user results for this part (after the survey, only if you can)

  1. Do this only if your questionnaire has items about the features of this part.
  2. Add a bar chart or table of the mean scores for those features.
  3. Write a paragraph that gives the mean, says where it falls on your interpretation scale from Chapter 3, and says what it means.
Sentence frame Figure 4.__ shows the mean score for [the feature]. The mean of [number] falls within [range], which is interpreted as [meaning]. This means that [what it says about the feature].
